# Pitch

## Tool: Vue.js
## Product: Calculus memorization mini-game

---

## Main pitch (1 min max)

Hi! Do you like math? Well whether or not you like math, my project is made to be useful to help your strengthen or grow your math skills, especially in AP calculus. Here's my website using Vue.js. There are 3 decks, 'unit circle', 'derivatives', and 'identities'. When you click a deck, there's a score and progress check up top, with questions below. If you get a question wrong, a sad sprite will show and 0.5 points are deducted. If you get it right, a cheeky sprite will show. You have infinite attempts to answer, aimed to help users memorize the right answer for the future. Anytime you want to exit a deck, just click on 'home' in the navbar. Do you have any questions?

## Potential common Q&A (30 secs max)

- How did you use your tool or decide to use it?
   - My tool was Vue.js, and a key way I used it was for toggling on and off HTML containers as you can see on my website. For example, clicking on a deck will enable a score and question container to pop up.
- Why did you choose Vue.js?
   - For me, I found vue.js more convenient on how I was able to use it on my school chromebook. At first, I was considering React.js but I couldn't find a way to use react.js on my school chromebook without struggling before having to settle on one tool. This led to me using vue.js, which actively allowed me to use it during and out of school hours, allowing me to work more efficiently and quicker.
- What problem does this solve that other apps don't?
   - For other apps, it's common that without paying your access to features are pretty limited. Sometimes, the practice they have isn't as targetted on more specific topics. This I found annoying, so I made this project to help myself and others that may want to use it too for free to improve their memory on AP calculus concepts.
- Couldn't a student just guess randomly until they get it right?
   - To me, repetition helps with memory, and I hope others feel the same way. Since once you get something wrong, you wonder what the right answer is. With my project, repeating the questions will help students recognize patterns and eventually keep these patterns memorized. 
- Does progress save between sessions?
   - Currently, there is no saved progress, but this is also intentional. My app is meant to be similar to flashcards, where you repeat questions numerous times until the concept sticks to your brain. 
- What would you add with more time?
   - With more time, I would definitely add more decks for other key calculus concepts to remember, as AP calculus does not give you a reference sheet. I would also want to try adding feedback (to go on-top of the infinite attempts) to help students significantly improve their understanding. The feedback would probably be a little pop up somewhere that won't obstruct view of the questions, giving a mini-explanation.
